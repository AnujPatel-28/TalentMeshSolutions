## Why the agency you pick matters more than the fee you negotiate

Most employers compare recruitment agencies on one number: the percentage of annual CTC they charge. It is the easiest thing to compare and the least useful. A cheaper agency that sends twenty unscreened CVs costs you more in interview hours than a costlier one that sends four candidates who can all do the job.

The real cost of a bad hiring partner is time. Every week a role stays open, the work either does not get done or gets absorbed by a team already at capacity. In Ahmedabad's tighter skill pools — mid-level engineering, pharma quality roles, specialist finance — a three-month vacancy is common when sourcing is weak.

So evaluate for capability first, and negotiate the fee second. Here is what to actually check.

One thing to settle before you start calling agencies: whether an agency is the right model at all. If you are hiring steadily and in volume, an internal recruiter or an outsourced hiring function may serve you better than per-role placement fees. We have compared the three options in [RPO vs staffing agency vs in-house recruiting](/blog/rpo-vs-staffing-agency-vs-in-house-recruitment) — worth ten minutes before you shortlist vendors.

## 1. Ask where their candidates come from

Any agency can log into a job portal and run a keyword search. That is not sourcing; that is filtering people who are already actively looking. The candidates you most want are usually employed, reasonably content, and not refreshing their profiles.

Ask directly:

- What share of your placements last year came from job portals versus your own network and referrals?
- How do you approach a passive candidate who is not applying to anything?
- For a niche role, how do you map which companies in Gujarat employ that skill set?

You are listening for a real answer about referral networks, industry mapping and direct outreach. Vagueness here predicts a CV dump later.

## 2. Make them explain their screening, step by step

"We screen thoroughly" means nothing. Ask them to walk you through what happens between a CV landing in their inbox and it reaching you.

A credible answer covers:

- A structured conversation with the candidate, not just a confirmation call
- Verification of current CTC, notice period and offers already in hand
- An honest read on why the candidate is leaving and what they actually want next
- Some assessment against your specific requirements, not generic role fit

Then ask for the single most useful artefact in this whole process: a sample candidate summary from a past mandate, with the personal details redacted. If the summary is three lines of resume paraphrase, their screening is three lines deep.

## 3. Agree what a realistic timeline looks like — before you start

Speed matters, but a promise of "CVs by tomorrow" for a specialist role should worry you, not reassure you. It usually means a portal search with no screening layer.

For most standard mandates, a screened shortlist within 48 to 72 hours of a signed-off requirement is a reasonable expectation. Niche, senior and confidential searches legitimately take longer, because the candidate universe is smaller and every approach is a one-to-one conversation. What matters is that your partner tells you the realistic timeline at intake, not three weeks later when you ask why nothing has arrived.

## 4. Check they understand your industry, not just recruitment

Ahmedabad and the wider Gujarat belt run on a specific mix: pharmaceuticals, chemicals, engineering and manufacturing, textiles, IT services, and a growing base of finance and back-office operations. Each has its own vocabulary, its own certifications, and its own idea of what "senior" means.

A recruiter who cannot tell the difference between a QA and a QC role in a pharma plant, or who reads five years of experience the same way in IT as in plant engineering, will waste your interview slots. Ask which roles in your sector they have closed, and what made those searches difficult. The difficulty question is the honest one — anyone can list job titles.

## 5. Read the replacement clause carefully

Every agency offers a replacement guarantee. The details decide whether it is worth anything.

Check:

- **Length of the window.** Measured from date of joining, and long enough to cover a genuine probation view.
- **What triggers it.** Does it apply if the candidate resigns, if you terminate for performance, or only if they never join at all?
- **What you get.** A free replacement search, a pro-rata refund, or a credit note against future hiring?
- **How many attempts.** Is the replacement itself covered by the same guarantee?
- **What voids it.** Role changes, salary revisions and internal restructuring are common exclusions.

Get the answers in the contract, not the pitch deck.

## 6. Understand the fee structure completely

Ask for the fee in writing with these points settled:

- Is the percentage calculated on fixed CTC or total CTC including variables and joining bonus?
- When is the invoice raised — on offer, on joining, or split across both?
- What are the payment terms, and is GST additional?
- Are there separate charges for background verification, assessments or bulk drives?
- For contract staffing, is the markup transparent and is statutory compliance included in it?

Two agencies quoting the same headline percentage can differ by a wide margin once the base and the add-ons are settled.

## 7. Confirm how compliance and verification are handled

If the agency runs contract or temporary staffing for you, they are handling statutory obligations on your behalf — provident fund, ESIC where applicable, and the documentation that goes with them. Ask what proof of remittance you receive each month. Non-compliance in a contractor's payroll has a way of becoming the principal employer's problem.

For permanent hiring, confirm whether background verification and reference checks are part of the service or billed separately, and what they cover — employment history, education, and professional references at minimum.

## 8. Meet the person who will actually run your mandate

Business development wins the account; a recruiter delivers it. Ask who owns your requirement day to day, how many mandates they carry at once, and how often you will hear from them when there is no good news to report.

The last part matters most. Agencies that only call when they have a CV to push are the ones that go quiet for a fortnight while your role sits open.

## A short due-diligence list you can run in a week

1. Ask for two client references in your industry and actually call them.
2. Request one redacted candidate summary from a past mandate.
3. Confirm the replacement clause in writing.
4. Get the full fee structure, including the calculation base and GST treatment.
5. Meet the delivery recruiter, not only the account manager.
6. Agree an intake format — role brief, must-haves versus nice-to-haves, interview panel and turnaround commitments — before the search starts.

That last point is the one employers skip most often, and it causes more failed searches than any other single factor. An agency briefed in a ten-minute phone call will deliver ten-minute-quality candidates.
