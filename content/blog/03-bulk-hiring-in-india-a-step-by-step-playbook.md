## Volume hiring fails in the logistics, not the sourcing

When a company needs three hundred people for a new site, the instinct is to worry about whether enough candidates exist. Usually they do. What breaks is everything around them: the assessment that takes four days to score, the interview panel with six slots a day against sixty candidates, the offer letters that go out a fortnight late, and the forty percent of accepted candidates who never turn up on day one.

Bulk hiring is an operations problem wearing a recruitment costume. Plan it that way and it becomes predictable.

This playbook assumes you have already decided who is running the drive — your own team, an agency, or an outsourced hiring function. If that is still open, settle it first: [RPO vs staffing agency vs in-house recruiting](/blog/rpo-vs-staffing-agency-vs-in-house-recruitment) covers the trade-offs at volume.

## Step 1: Do the funnel maths backwards

Start from joiners, not applicants. Work backwards through every stage with an honest conversion assumption, and you will discover the top-of-funnel number you actually need.

The chain looks like this:

1. Joiners needed
2. Offers required — allowing for offer-to-joining drop-off
3. Selections required — allowing for candidates who decline
4. Final interviews required
5. Assessment passes required
6. Candidates screened
7. Applications or database contacts needed

If you have run a similar drive before, use your own conversion rates at each step. If you have not, take conservative assumptions, write them down, and correct them after week one — the point is to have explicit numbers you can revise, rather than a vague sense that "we should get enough people".

Two stages deserve special attention because they are where volume drives quietly die:

- **Screening to assessment.** For entry-level and high-volume roles, this is where the largest absolute drop happens.
- **Offer to joining.** In competitive markets and for entry-level roles, drop-off here can be severe. Plan for it explicitly instead of treating each no-show as a surprise.

## Step 2: Define the role tightly, then hold the line

Volume hiring rewards a narrow, unambiguous specification. Before sourcing begins, settle:

- The minimum qualification, and whether it is genuinely a minimum
- Experience band, expressed as a range with a hard floor
- Language requirements, specified by skill — read, write, speak, and at what level
- Shift and location expectations, stated plainly
- The compensation band, including allowances and any shift premium
- The two or three attributes that actually predict performance in the role

Then stop adding criteria. Every additional must-have narrows the pool multiplicatively, and in a volume drive the pool is the whole game. Keep a separate list of nice-to-haves for tie-breaking.

The compensation band matters more than anything else on this list. A band set below the local market means your funnel leaks at every stage and no amount of sourcing effort compensates.

## Step 3: Build a screen that scales

You cannot personally interview two thousand people. The screening layer has to do most of the work, and it has to do it consistently.

What works at volume:

- **A structured phone or video screen** with a fixed question set and a simple scoring sheet, so that ten recruiters produce comparable judgments.
- **A short standardised assessment** — aptitude, language, typing speed, domain basics, whatever the role genuinely needs. Keep it under about thirty minutes; longer tests increase drop-off far more than they increase signal.
- **Automatic knockouts** on non-negotiables — location, shift willingness, notice period — asked early rather than discovered at the interview stage.
- **A single interview round wherever possible.** Two rounds for a high-volume role doubles your scheduling problem and halves your conversion.

Score the assessment the same day. Volume candidates are usually interviewing in several places at once, and a four-day scoring turnaround costs you your best candidates rather than your weakest.

## Step 4: Plan the drive as an event

Whether you run walk-ins, a campus drive or a virtual assessment day, treat it as event management with a run sheet.

**Before the day**

- Confirm venue capacity against expected turnout, allowing for the fact that a meaningful share of registrations will not arrive
- Assign named panellists to named time blocks, in writing
- Prepare registration, document verification, assessment and interview as four separate physical stations
- Print more forms than you need, and have offline backups for anything that depends on connectivity
- Send reminders the evening before and the morning of the drive — this single step materially improves turnout
- Tell candidates exactly what to bring: identity proof, qualification documents, previous employment papers, photographs

**On the day**

- Own the queue. A candidate waiting three hours becomes a candidate who leaves, and tells others.
- Keep one person as floor coordinator whose only job is flow, not interviewing.
- Record outcomes as they happen, not from memory at the end of the day.
- Aim to communicate results the same day or the next morning. Same-day offers are the single strongest lever on conversion in volume hiring.

**After the day**

- Log every candidate, including rejections, with the reason. Today's near-miss for one role is next month's strong fit for another.
- Reconcile registered, attended, assessed, interviewed and selected numbers. These are your conversion rates for the next drive.

## Step 5: Manage the offer-to-joining gap deliberately

This is where volume hiring is won or lost, and where most companies do nothing at all. Between offer and joining date, a candidate is being courted by everyone else who screened them.

What reduces drop-off:

- **Compress the gap.** Every additional week between offer and joining raises the chance of losing the candidate.
- **Stay in contact.** A short check-in each week from a named person, not an automated mailer.
- **Front-load the paperwork.** Documentation collected early surfaces problems while you can still act on them.
- **Confirm the joining logistics.** Reporting time, location, whom to ask for, what to carry. Confusion on day one reads as disorganisation.
- **Over-offer knowingly.** If your historical drop-off is a third, make offers accordingly — but track it, because over-offering without tracking creates its own problems.
- **Keep a live waitlist.** Selected-but-not-offered candidates, contacted quickly, fill gaps far faster than reopening sourcing.

## Step 6: Track five numbers, not twenty

Volume drives generate endless data. These five tell you whether the drive is working while you can still change it:

1. **Screen-to-select rate.** Falling? Your sourcing or your specification is off.
2. **Assessment pass rate.** Very low means the test or the target profile is wrong; very high means the test is not filtering.
3. **Interview show-up rate.** Low means slow scheduling or weak candidate engagement.
4. **Offer acceptance rate.** Low points at compensation or at how the role is being described.
5. **Joining rate against offers.** Your bottom line, and the only one that pays for the drive.

Review these weekly during an active drive. Monthly reporting on a six-week drive tells you what went wrong after it has finished going wrong.

## What tends to go wrong

- **Sourcing before specifying.** Ambiguous criteria produce a funnel nobody can evaluate.
- **Panel capacity assumed, not booked.** Interviewers have day jobs. Blocked calendars are the only real commitment.
- **Compensation set from last year's benchmark.** Entry-level bands move quickly in competitive markets.
- **No same-day decisions.** In volume hiring, slow beats you more often than picky does.
- **Rejected candidates discarded.** You are rebuilding the same database next quarter.
- **Compliance treated as an afterthought.** Documentation, verification and statutory registration for a large cohort takes real time — start it in parallel, not after joining. If the cohort is going onto a contractor's payroll rather than yours, the [compliance position is worth reading properly](/blog/contract-and-flexi-staffing-in-india) before the drive, not after it.
