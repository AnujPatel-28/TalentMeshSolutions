Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Privacy Policy — India 
This Privacy Policy is a DPDP-first, India-specific template designed to align with the Digital Personal 
Data Protection Act, 2023 and the rules made thereunder. It is intended to function both as a publishable 
privacy policy for Data Fiduciaries and as a practical reference document, supported by guidance notes 
that explain statutory intent, scope, and operational considerations. The document has been drafted as 
a standalone DPDP policy, rather than as a retrofit of GDPR-style privacy notices, and is structured to 
be DPDP-native, industry-neutral, and modular, with optional annexures for sector-specific 
requirements. Where relevant, guidance notes explain why a clause exists, which statutory provision it 
reflects, and how it may be interpreted or operationalised in practice. This document is relevant for 
organisations operating in India, as well as for legal, compliance, privacy, product, and governance 
teams responsible for implementing DPDP compliance.  
Important note: This document is a general template and guidance aid only. It does not constitute legal 
advice and should be adapted based on the nature and scale of processing, sector-specific laws, and 
applicable government notifications.

1. INTRODUCTION & SCOPE 
______________ (the “Company”) respects the privacy of individuals and is committed to protecting 
personal data. This Privacy Policy explains how personal data is collected, used, disclosed, stored, and 
otherwise processed by the Company in accordance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 
(“DPDP Act”) and the rules made thereunder. 
This Policy applies to all individuals whose personal data is processed by the Company, whether 
through its websites, mobile applications, products, services, platforms, or other interactions, online or 
offline, where applicable. 
This Policy should be read together with any specific notices, consent requests, or disclosures provided 
at the time of collection of personal data. 
Guidance Note: This introductory section sets the scope of the policy and establishes that the document 
is a DPDP specific privacy notice, not a generic information policy. DPDP requires clear notice to 
Data Principals regarding how their personal data is processed (Section 5), and this section frames the 
document accordingly. 
2. APPLICABILITY & DEFINITIONS 
For the purposes of this Privacy Policy: 
• 
“Data Principal” means the individual to whom the personal data relates. 
• 
“Data Fiduciary” means the Company, which determines the purpose and means of processing 
personal data. 
• 
“Data Processor” means any person who processes personal data on behalf of the Company. 
• 
“Personal Data” means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such 
data. 
Capitalised terms not defined herein shall have the meaning assigned to them under the DPDP Act.

Personal data is processed only where one or more lawful grounds under the DPDP Act apply, including

Guidance Note: The DPDP Act does not use the GDPR concept of “lawful bases” in a granular manner. 
Instead, Section 4 permits processing either on the basis of consent or for specified “legitimate uses” 
enumerated under the Act. This section therefore focuses on purpose limitation and transparency, which 
are core DPDP principles, while avoiding over-categorisation of legal bases that DPDP does not 
require. 
6. CONSENT, WITHDRAWAL & CONSEQUENCES 
Where processing of personal data is based on consent, such consent shall be: 
• free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous; and 
• provided through a clear affirmative action by the Data Principal. 
The Data Principal may withdraw consent at any time through the mechanism specified in the notice or 
otherwise made available by the Company. Withdrawal of consent shall take effect prospectively and 
shall not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out prior to such withdrawal. 
Upon withdrawal of consent, the Company may be unable to provide certain products, services, or 
functionalities, and such consequences shall be communicated to the Data Principal at the time of 
seeking consent or withdrawal. 
Guidance Note: This section reflects Section 6 of the DPDP Act, which governs consent standards, 
withdrawal, and consequences. DPDP requires that withdrawal be as easy as giving consent and that 
Data Principals be informed of the consequences of withdrawal.  The DPDP Act also recognises 
registered Consent Managers as optional intermediaries for managing consent; where a Data Fiduciary 
engages a Consent Manager, additional disclosures may be required.  
7. PROCESSING OF CHILDREN’S PERSONAL DATA  
Where the Company processes personal data of a child, such processing shall be carried out only with 
verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian and in accordance with applicable requirements 
under the DPDP Act and Rules. The Company shall take reasonable efforts to verify the age of the Data 
Principal and, where applicable, the authenticity of the parent or lawful guardian providing consent, 
using mechanisms proportionate to the nature of the processing. The Company shall not undertake 
processing of children’s personal data that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of 
a child, including tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising, where such restrictions 
apply. This section applies only where the Company’s products, services, or platforms are directed at 
children or knowingly collect personal data of children. 
Guidance Note: This section implements Section 9 of the DPDP Act, which imposes heightened 
obligations for processing children’s personal data. DPDP adopts a strict protective approach for 
children, with limited exceptions that may be notified by the Government. Because these obligations are 
not universally applicable, the policy expressly flags conditional applicability rather than assuming 
child-directed processing.

9. CROSS-BORDER TRANSFERS OF PERSONAL DATA 
The Company may transfer personal data outside India only in accordance with the provisions of the 
DPDP Act and applicable rules or notifications issued by the Central Government. Personal data shall 
not be transferred to any country or territory that is notified by the Central Government as a restricted 
or prohibited destination for such transfers. Where cross-border transfer of personal data is permitted, 
the Company shall ensure that such transfers are undertaken only for lawful purposes and subject to 
appropriate safeguards. The Company shall promptly review and comply with any Government 
notification or direction that restricts, conditions, or prohibits cross-border transfers of personal data. 
Guidance Note: This section reflects Section 16 of the DPDP Act, which empowers the Central 
Government to restrict or prohibit the transfer of personal data to specified countries or territories. The 
procedure for issuing such restrictions is set out under Rule 15 of the DPDP Rules. Unlike GDPR, the 
DPDP framework does not mandate standard contractual clauses or predefined transfer mechanisms. 
Instead, it adopts a notification-based, sovereign control model. Accordingly, this policy avoids 
committing to specific transfer instruments and remains aligned with future Government directions.
